M.A.R.K. I answered whether a long-term personal assistant could exist as a modular system
independent of one AI model. It proved that independent repositories, a composition runtime,
native realtime audio, memory, state, and bounded tools could form one usable system.

M.A.R.K. II asks a different question:

> How capable can an assistant become with the current generation of half-duplex voice models?

That makes Mark II an active development line rather than a count of completed features. The
voice model is the realtime interaction layer of a larger platform. It can answer directly, use a
bounded tool, delegate deeper work, or continue a long-running operation while the platform owns
the lifecycle around it.

The generation remains half-duplex. Mark III is reserved for a future conversational model that
can process both directions continuously and decide when to speak. Mark II prepares provider-neutral
event and lifecycle boundaries for that future without claiming to implement it now.
